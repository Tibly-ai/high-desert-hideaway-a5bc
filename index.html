The place

A small cabin that earns its keep after dark.

High Desert Hideaway sits on an acre of creosote and boulders in the Yucca Mesa flats, past where the pavement gives out. The nearest neighbor is far enough that you can hear the wind change.

Days here get spent somewhere else: Joshua Tree, the Integratron, a long lunch in Pioneertown. Nights get spent right here. Someone racks the table, someone drags the cooler out to the cowboy pool, and the fire pit gets lit around the time the sky turns orange.

We stocked it the way we’d want it stocked. Good coffee, real cast iron, extra blankets for the cold nights that surprise people, and a water bowl by the back door.

The particulars

Everything, listed plainly.

No surprises on arrival. Here is exactly what you get.

Sleeping
Sleeps 4 across two bedrooms. Front room has a king with a wool blanket and blackout curtain; back room has two twins that push together into a king if you ask. No sofa bed, no air mattress in a closet.
Bath
One full bathroom with a deep soaking tub, tiled shower, and a heater that actually keeps up in January. Towels, beach towels for the pool, and a hair dryer are stocked.
Cowboy pool
A 10-foot galvanized stock tank on the back patio, filled and treated before every stay. Sun-warm by mid-afternoon from about April through October. It is not heated, and we drain it in deep winter.
Pool table
A full-size slate table in the living room, recovered in green felt, with four cues, a bridge, and chalk that gets replaced when it runs low. House rule: winner racks.
Fire pit
Stone ring out past the patio with four canvas chairs and a first bundle of firewood on us. Subject to San Bernardino County burn restrictions; we text you the current status the morning you arrive.
Kitchen
Gas range, full fridge, drip coffee and a French press, cast iron skillets, a Dutch oven, and enough plates for six. Grocery run: Stater Bros in Yucca Valley, about nine minutes.
Working
Fiber internet with a desk and proper chair in the back bedroom by the window. Cell service is spotty for some carriers, so calls go better on wifi.
Dogs
Two dogs welcome, no extra fee. Fenced back yard, water bowls, and towels by the door for sandy paws. We ask that they stay off the beds and out of the cowboy pool.
Views & outside
One acre, unobstructed, facing the Little San Bernardino range. Covered porch on the west side for sunsets, no streetlights, and dark enough to see the Milky Way most clear nights.
Also included
Central heat and A/C, washer and dryer, free driveway parking for two vehicles, board games, a record player with a stack of country and soul, and outdoor string lights.
Not here
No hot tub, no pool heater, no elevator or step-free entry (there are two porch steps), and no smoking anywhere on the property.
Times
Check-in 4pm, check-out 11am. Messages answered daily 8am to 8pm.

Who it suits

Four people, one long weekend.

Most stays here run Friday to Monday. These are the ones that keep coming back.

01

Two couples, no plans

The layout works because the bedrooms sit on opposite ends of the cabin with the bathroom between. Everybody gets their own morning.

02

Road trips with the dog

Fenced yard, no pet fee, and a list of the dog-friendly patios in town taped inside the kitchen cabinet. Joshua Tree trails do not allow dogs, so we mark the ones nearby that do.

03

Slow weeks in winter

Fiber internet, a real desk, and cold clear afternoons. Stays of seven nights or longer get a reduced rate; ask when you write.
