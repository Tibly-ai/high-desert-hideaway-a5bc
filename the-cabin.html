Yucca Valley · The tour

Two bedrooms
at the end of
a dirt road

High Desert Hideaway is a 1,050 square foot cabin on two and a half acres of creosote and Joshua trees. One room for the pool table, one for cooking and eating, two for sleeping. Everything else happens outside.

We bought it in 2016 with a sagging porch and a swamp cooler held together by wire. Since then it has gotten a new roof, a real kitchen, central heat and air, and a fence around the whole back half so the dogs can run. The rest we left alone on purpose: plank floors, a wood stove that still smells like mesquite, and a sky that goes fully dark by nine.

Spec sheet

The particulars, written out plainly

No surprises on arrival. This is the whole property, room by room, including the things we don't have.

01  Sleeps
4 guests, two bedrooms, one bath. We hold to four overnight, no exceptions, because the septic and the well are sized for it.
02  Beds
King bed in the front bedroom, queen in the back. Both get blackout curtains, wool blankets and cotton percale sheets. There is no sofa bed and no air mattress.
03  Bath
One full bathroom with a tiled walk-in shower, plus a separate outdoor shower on the west wall with hot water and a slatted cedar floor.
04  Pool table
A 7-foot slate table in the great room with room to shoot on all four sides. Two full cues, one short cue, fresh tips, chalk in the corner pocket tray. House rule: winner racks.
05  Cowboy pool
An 8-foot galvanized stock tank on the south patio, filled and treated April through October, about two feet deep. Shaded by a slatted ramada from noon on. Covered and drained in winter.
06  Fire pit
Steel ring set in gravel with four Adirondack chairs around it. First bundle of firewood is on us, more in the shed. We close the pit whenever the county issues a burn ban, and we will tell you before you arrive.
07  Kitchen
Full kitchen: gas range, full-size fridge, dishwasher, cast iron, a stocked spice drawer, drip coffee and a French press. Salt, oil, coffee and filters are here when you get here.
08  Outside
2.5 acres, with the back half fully fenced at five feet. Covered porch with a swing, shaded dining table for six, hammock, propane grill and a pair of camp chairs for wherever you want to sit.
09  Pets
Two dogs welcome, any size, no breed rules. Bowls, a spare leash, towels for muddy paws and a shaded water station by the porch steps. One flat pet fee per stay, shown at checkout.
10  Workspace
A desk under the north window with a real chair, a power strip and a window view of the ridge. Fiber internet, tested at 180 Mbps down, strong enough for video calls all day.
11  Views
Open desert to the south and west, Sawtooth ridgeline on the horizon, no neighbor within sight of the fire pit. Dark enough at night to pick out the Milky Way from a lawn chair.
12  Getting here
About 15 minutes to the west entrance of Joshua Tree National Park, 10 minutes to the grocery store on Twentynine Palms Highway, 45 minutes to Palm Springs airport. The last quarter mile is graded dirt. A sedan handles it fine.
13  Not here
No hot tub, no swimming pool, no laundry, no cable. Cell service is spotty on most carriers, so use the wifi for calls. Mail-order deliveries do not reach the road reliably.

Are dogs actually welcome, or just tolerated?
Welcome. Two dogs, any size, no breed restrictions. The back half of the property is fenced at five feet, there are bowls and towels in the mudroom, and the floors are plank and tile for a reason. We ask that dogs stay off the beds and are not left alone in the cabin for long stretches, since a strange house makes some dogs loud.
How does check-in work?
Self check-in with a lockbox on the porch post. You get the code and driving notes the morning of arrival, including a photo of the turn off the paved road, because the mailbox is easy to miss after dark. Early check-in is often possible on weekdays. Just ask.
When is the cowboy pool filled?
April through October. It sits under a slatted ramada, so it holds a swimmable temperature through the hot months and cools off fast at night. From November to March it is drained and covered, and we swap the ramada shade for extra firewood.
Can we have friends over or host an event?
Four overnight guests, and up to six people on the property during the day if you let us know in advance. No parties, no weddings, no photo or film shoots. Quiet hours run from 10pm to 8am. Sound travels a long way out here, and our neighbors have been good to us.
Is there firewood, and can we always use the fire pit?
The first bundle is included and more is stacked in the shed. During San Bernardino County burn bans the pit is closed, usually in late summer. We check the status before every arrival and send word, and the propane grill stays available either way.
What is the road like?
Paved to within a quarter mile, then graded dirt to the gate. Any car handles it at a slow speed. After a rare hard rain there can be a soft patch or two, and we will text you if that is the case.
How is the internet and cell service?
Fiber internet at roughly 180 Mbps, reliable enough to work a full week from the desk. Cell reception is weak on most carriers inside the cabin, so switch on wifi calling before you settle in.
What is your cancellation policy?
Full refund up to 14 days before check-in, half back up to 7 days. Inside a week we do our best to rebook the nights and refund what we can. Book direct and we will always talk it through with you first.
